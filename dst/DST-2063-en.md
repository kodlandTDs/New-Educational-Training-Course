## Section 2 — Knowledge check (12 questions, 1 point each)

### Q1. Lesson 1. How do your students load the .sb3 template into Creator Lab?

- A. By double-clicking the file
- B. **Via the "Upload" option** ✅
- C. By dragging it onto the stage
- D. From the Scratch website

> *Feedback:* Always via "Upload", never a double click. This is the single most common failure of Lesson 1.

### Q2. Lesson 1. A student asks how to make a counter with a loop. What do you do?

- A. Teach loops and variables — they are clearly ready
- B. Tell them it cannot be done in Scratch
- C. Ask AI Buddy to write the loop for them
- D. **Keep the focus: variables, loops and complex conditions are deliberately not taught yet** ✅

> *Feedback:* The lesson narrows the focus on purpose. Variables arrive in Lesson 2 as the crystal counter; compound conditions come later.

### Q3. Lesson 1. What must the AI-generated background satisfy?

- A. **It must be free of characters and leave central space for the character** ✅
- B. It must include a character so the scene looks alive
- C. It must be black and white so the sprite stands out
- D. It must be generated twice so the student can choose

> *Feedback:* No characters, and central space left for the sprite. Remember too that publishing on Sparks and sending the link on the Kodland platform are two separate steps.

### Q4. Lesson 2. What must the AI-generated crystal costume satisfy?

- A. It must include the hero in the frame
- B. Opaque background, size 100%
- C. **Transparent background, size set to 25%** ✅
- D. It must be drawn by hand, not generated

> *Feedback:* Transparent background and 25% size. The crystal is the one and only AI step of this lesson.

### Q5. Lesson 2. The crystals are built across three increasing levels of autonomy. What is the correct order?

- A. **Connect ready blocks → complete what is missing → build the sequence alone** ✅
- B. Watch → copy → repeat
- C. Plan → code → test
- D. Prototype → debug → publish

> *Feedback:* Connect, Complete, Build it yourself — the same scaffolding ladder used across all four courses. Let them build the third crystal unaided before you intervene.

### Q6. Lesson 2. What must the green flag do?

- A. Only return the hero to its starting position
- B. **Return the hero to its position AND reset the score to zero** ✅
- C. Only reset the score to zero
- D. Reload the whole project from the template

> *Feedback:* Both. Verify the start script contains the hero position and the score reset before you let anyone publish.

### Q7. Lesson 3. While the portal is still hidden, what must be true?

- A. It must react to being touched so students can test it
- B. It must already show the decorated WIN screen
- C. **It must not react to being touched — the transition to WIN is a separate step** ✅
- D. It must be deleted and recreated in Lesson 4

> *Feedback:* A hidden portal that reacts to touch is the classic bug here. The WIN transition is configured in its own step, and the WIN screen stays intentionally empty until Lesson 4.

### Q8. Lesson 3. A student's key appears but the portal never opens. What do you check first?

- A. Whether the crystal counter reached 3
- B. Whether the hero is touching the portal sprite
- C. Whether the backdrop was generated with AI
- D. **Whether the message the key sends and the one the portal receives match exactly** ✅

> *Feedback:* The broadcast names must match exactly. That mismatch is the usual cause of a key that appears but a portal that does nothing.

### Q9. Lesson 3. A student says their game is too hard. What are they allowed to change?

- A. The code of the collection mechanic
- B. Nothing — the difficulty is fixed by the template
- C. The number of crystals required, from 3 to 1
- D. **The position of the objects, not the code** ✅

> *Feedback:* Difficulty is balanced by moving danger or key, not by rewriting logic or adding new mechanics. Let the student make that call themselves.

### Q10. Lesson 4. What is the rule about the say block and Text to Speech?

- A. Use both together for accessibility
- B. Use Text to Speech only, say is deprecated
- C. **Use only one, never both** ✅
- D. Use say only, Text to Speech is not available

> *Feedback:* Use one or the other, never both at once. And only one AI backdrop is allowed in this lesson.

### Q11. Lesson 4. Where does the victory-phrase block go?

- A. On the Stage, before the backdrop switch
- B. **On the Portal sprite, right after the backdrop switch to WIN** ✅
- C. On the hero sprite, at the start of the game
- D. Anywhere — the position does not matter

> *Feedback:* On the Portal sprite, right after the switch to WIN. Putting it on the Stage is the classic bug of this lesson.

### Q12. Lesson 4. How should the "Iteration Award" be given?

- A. **For style or a unique detail, without comparing projects to each other** ✅
- B. To the best project, so the group has a clear standard to aim for
- C. To whoever finished first
- D. By student vote at the end of the mini-festival

> *Feedback:* Never comparative. It recognises a personal decision — style or a unique detail — and closes a module the group should celebrate explicitly.
